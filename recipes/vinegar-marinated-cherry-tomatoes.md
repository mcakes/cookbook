ingredients:
  - 700 g mixed cherry tomatoes (buy extra to allow for culls)
  - 60 ml sherry vinegar
  - 60 ml cold water
  - 1 1/2 tsp sugar
  - 1 tsp fine salt
  - 1/2 tsp coriander seed
  - 1/4 tsp fennel seed
  - 1 garlic clove, smashed
  - 1 wide strip lemon zest
  - 4 basil stems (leaves reserved)
  - 2 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
  - 1/2 English cucumber, to serve
  - 1/4 cup basil or mint leaves, torn, to serve
  - Flaky salt and best finishing olive oil, to serve
---

## Method

1. Bring a pot of water to a boil and set up an ice bath. Score a shallow X in the base of each cherry tomato. Work in batches of ten or so: 15 seconds in the boiling water, straight into the ice. The skins slip off with a pinch. Discard any that split badly. Aim for 8 to 10 clean tomatoes per person.
2. Toast the coriander and fennel seed in a dry pan for about 30 seconds, until they smell nutty.
3. Combine the toasted seeds with the sherry vinegar, cold water, sugar, salt, smashed garlic, lemon zest, basil stems and olive oil in a bowl. Whisk until the sugar and salt dissolve. It should taste assertively sour, since the tomatoes will dilute it.
4. Add the peeled tomatoes, turn gently to coat, cover and refrigerate at least 1 hour, turning once partway through. Three hours is better; they hold overnight.
5. Seed the cucumber and cut into small dice or shave into ribbons. Toss with a pinch of salt and leave in a sieve to drain for 15 minutes, then pat dry.
6. Lift the tomatoes out of the marinade with a slotted spoon. Serve very cold with the cucumber, a spoonful of the marinade, torn herbs, flaky salt and a good pour of olive oil.

## Notes

Peeling is the whole point. Skin blocks the marinade, so unpeeled cherry tomatoes taste seasoned rather than infused, and they resist instead of bursting. Because they are peeled, a cold marinade is enough — a hot brine would slump them.

Swapping half the sherry vinegar for verjus gives a softer, grapier acidity if straight sherry reads too aggressive.

**Serving over ajo blanco.** These were built as a first course spooned over chilled [Ajo Blanco](ajo-blanco.md), which is how to serve them if you want the acid to land on something. Pool about 120 ml of the cold soup in each shallow bowl, arrange the tomatoes and cucumber on top, spoon over a little marinade, and finish with the herbs, toasted Marcona almonds, olive oil and flaky salt. Both recipes serve 4 as written and are entirely make-ahead. Straight tomatoes with no soup under them are also good on grilled bread, alongside a soft cheese, or folded through a grain salad — but without that fat to buffer it, pull the vinegar back to about 45 ml.
